Dough
Why this hydration?
Miller & Baker absorbs more water (75%), Caputo Pizzeria less (65%), most others sit at 72%. Your blend lands in between. Wetter dough → more open crumb, harder to shape. Drier → easier to handle, tighter crumb.
Why 30% poolish?
30% pre-fermented flour balances flavour complexity against structure. Below 20%: too little poolish acid to develop character. Above 40%: excess acid degrades gluten. 30% is Hamelman's baguette baseline: a one-day poolish at room temp delivers complex flavour without weakening the final dough.
Environment
Advanced · flour tuning▾
What is τ (tau)?
τ is the Newton's-law cooling time constant for your container, measuring how fast the dough core reaches ambient temperature. Lower τ = faster. Formula for warm-up time: t = τ × ln((Tamb − Tfridge) / (Tamb − Ttarget)). Metal bowl (τ=60) conducts heat quickly; glass (τ=90) slower; a tall plastic tub (τ=111) slowest due to insulating walls and poor surface-to-volume ratio.
Why does fold count vary?
Set by your dominant flour's gluten character. Strong, elastic flours (Miller & Baker) need only 2 coil folds, as the tight network builds quickly and over-folding makes it too stiff. Extensible flours (Caputo Pizzeria) need 4 folds (2 stretch-and-fold + 2 coil) to develop structure progressively. Fewer folds → the coil folds from the tail of the sequence are kept (highest structural value).
Why this fermentolyse time?
Fermentolyse lets the gluten hydrate and relax before salt and bassinage are added, cutting mixing time by ~60%. GLOBAL baseline is 45 min. Miller & Baker targets 60 min: stoneground bran absorbs water slowly and the tight network needs longer to relax. Visual cue: dough shaggy at the start, a smooth window panel forming by the end.
Why 20 min minimum bench-rest?
Gluten relaxation is time-limited, not just temperature-limited. Below ~20 min the network won't fully release regardless of temperature, and final shaping will tear the dough. The formula (t = 40 × 2^((17−T)/10)) gives longer rests at colder temps but is always floored here. Miller & Baker's tight network benefits from 28+ min; that's why its flour profile sets the floor higher.
Gentle / Standard / Intensive
Gentle: coil folds only, no aggressive handling. Best for strong elastic flours (Miller & Baker) that build structure easily and tear if over-worked.

Standard: the scheduled stretch-and-fold + coil-fold sequence. Suits most flour blends.

Intensive: begin with ~100 slap-and-folds before the first scheduled fold, until the dough is smooth and shiny. For extensible, lower-protein Italian flours (Caputo Pizzeria) that need mechanical development to form a network.
Why diastatic malt?
Diastatic malt adds active α-amylase enzymes that break starch into fermentable sugars. At 0.5% it boosts crust browning (more reducing sugars for the Maillard reaction) and feeds gas production in the final proof. Caputo Pizzeria has very low natural enzyme activity, so malt compensates. Don't exceed 0.5% of flour: over-enzymed dough becomes sticky and slack and won't hold its shape.

Cold Retard vs Same Day

Cold Retard

  • More complex, nutty flavour
  • Better crust blistering & browning
  • More open, irregular crumb
  • Cold dough = easier to shape
  • Takes 2 days total

Same Day

  • Good baguettes, milder wheaty flavour
  • Decent crust, less blistering
  • Tighter, more even crumb
  • Warm dough = stickier to shape
  • Done in ~5–6 hrs after poolish
