## Overall Impression

A genuinely capable, domain-specific recipe app with real personality. The physics-derived timing and the flour-profile cascade set it apart from any template. The main thing holding it back is a fragmented help system: users encounter three different patterns to get contextual information, and the inconsistency is felt most acutely in the kitchen. Fix the disclosure pattern and this scores 32+.

## What's Working

1. **The physics-derived cascade** — seeing your actual kitchen temperature reflected in concrete, personalised times makes the recipe feel alive. The source pills make this derivation transparent without cluttering the UI. This is the app's core differentiator and it's executed well.
2. **The tip content quality** — DDT formula walkthrough, poke test table, bassinage note, 1.8% salt rationale. The app treats the user as intelligent and the tips reward curiosity at exactly the right moments without overwhelming by default. Heuristic 10 is a genuine 4/4.
3. **The "Oven Spring" hero animation** — sets a strong anticipatory emotional high immediately. Before the user has touched a control, they feel "I will make something alive and beautiful." Excellent peak-emotion anchoring.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Three competing disclosure patterns create an incoherent help system**
- **Why it matters**: Users learn interaction patterns by repetition. When contextual help uses a ⓘ modal for Method, inline collapsibles for 8 other controls, and a separate accordion header for Schedule, the mental model never stabilises. On a phone with floury hands, a user who taps ⓘ expecting an inline dropdown (because that's how all the other tips work) and gets a modal is disoriented. Cognitive overhead doubles.
- **Fix**: Adopt info-btn (ⓘ → modal sheet) as the **single** disclosure pattern for all contextual "why" information. Convert the 8 tip collapsibles to ⓘ buttons on their relevant labels; content moves into modals reusing the existing `.modal-overlay` infrastructure. Exception: the Schedule accordion stays as a collapsible panel because it contains interactive controls (the time input), not read-only help — that's a different pattern class.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] Blend slider sits in ambiguous visual position with no label**
- **Why it matters**: The slider sits between Flour B and Hydration with no label. Scanning top-to-bottom: Flour A, Flour B, [unlabelled slider], Hydration. Users may misread the slider as controlling hydration. On mobile where controls are tightly stacked this causes misoperations.
- **Fix**: Add a small `Flour blend` label row above the slider with a live ratio display: "85% A / 15% B", updating as the slider moves.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Bulk fermentation step leaves the user without a visual anchor at the highest-stakes moment**
- **Why it matters**: "Into the fridge at 30–50% expanded" gives no inline image. The visual cue tip exists but requires a proactive tap at a stressful, hands-busy moment. This is where the most errors occur in real baking.
- **Fix**: Add a brief inline sentence directly in the step body: "→ Ready when domed, jiggly, bubbles at edges, 30–50% larger than start" — before the fold table. Don't rely solely on the collapsible tip.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] No end-of-recipe celebration beat**
- **Why it matters**: Peak-end rule: the final memory of an experience strongly shapes overall satisfaction. The last visible element is "Cool on wire rack 20 min before cutting" — functional, emotionally flat. After hours of process, there's no acknowledgement of what was accomplished.
- **Fix**: After the final bake step, add a brief closing beat — minimal copy ("You made baguettes.") in accent colour, maybe a small static baguette motif. One element, not a banner.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable delight`

**[P3] Flour dropdown jargon blocks first-timers at the first decision**
- **Why it matters**: "Caputo Nuvola (13%)" vs "Strong Bread (12–13%)" forces a technical decision at setup before the user has context. Protein percentages help experts, cause paralysis for beginners.
- **Fix**: Add brief plain-language descriptors: "Miller & Baker Plain Flour · local, thirsty" / "Caputo Pizzeria Blue · extensible, chewy" / "Strong Bread Flour · reliable, widely available".
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: (1) Tip collapsibles require a precise tap on small label text — floury fingers will miss or activate adjacent controls. The ⓘ button pattern, being a distinct circular target, is easier to hit deliberately. (2) Stage bar pills are 0.68rem — very small for thumb navigation. (3) Marking a step done requires tapping on the step body with no clear "tap here" affordance. (4) Schedule section is collapsed by default — Casey in a rush may scroll past it without realising times are inside.

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**: (1) The first decision after loading is flour type — two dropdowns with technical names and protein percentages, no "I just have plain flour" path. (2) "Poolish % 30" has no explanation at the point of control. (3) "DDT 24°C" in the Step 2 formula-meta line is not labelled — Jordan sees "24°C" with no context that DDT means target dough temperature. (4) "Retard" as a method option is unknown vocabulary — may read as a negative word and hesitate.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**: (1) Tip collapsible divs have `tabindex=0` and keyboard handlers but no `role="button"` — screen readers announce "group" not "button". (2) `.info-btn` has `title='Compare methods'` but no `aria-label` — VoiceOver reads "? Compare methods". (3) Stage pill `<span>` elements use onclick with no `role="button"`. (4) Stepper buttons for folds lack aria-labels present on quantity/weight steppers. (5) Schedule `::after` pseudo-element for "+/−" toggle state is invisible to assistive technology.

## Minor Observations

- `.formula-line` text in the sticky terracotta header at small size with opacity 0.88 may fail WCAG AA — should be verified against rendered colour value
- Blend slider is redundant on mobile where `pctA`/`pctB` steppers are the primary input; the slider's iOS thumb is not customisable via `accent-color` and can be too small to drag reliably
- Schedule `.collapsible-header` `::after` "+/−" is not announced by screen readers — toggle state invisible to AT
- Modal overlay uses `display:none` → `display:flex` with no transition, feeling slightly jarring compared to the rest of the animation system
- "Minimum bench rest (min)" is the longest control label — worth verifying wrap behaviour at 320px viewport
- `shortFlour()` truncation applies inconsistently in the src pill between single-flour and blend states

## Questions to Consider

- The hero shows "72% hydration" as the dominant number, but in kitchen use the most actionable figure is probably water temperature. Should the hero be rethought around the *next actionable number* rather than a configuration summary?
- The app silently restores saved preferences — what happens if a returning user's `foldOverride=6` was saved under a flour that recommended 2 but they've since switched flour? Is silent restoration the right choice, or should there be a quiet "settings loaded from last time" nudge?
- Every tip collapsible shifts layout below it when opened — have you tested whether opening a tip while Advanced is also open pushes recipe output far enough down that users lose visual context of what they were configuring?
